Looking for a cattle uncertainty calculator for emissions rather than profitability? That is what this is. This is an emissions and greenhouse-gas inventory tool: it calculates the statistical uncertainty (confidence intervals and margins of error) around reported cattle methane and nitrous oxide emissions. It is not a breakeven, cost-of-gain or herd profitability calculator.

How do I quantify uncertainty in my national livestock GHG inventory?

When a country reports cattle greenhouse-gas emissions under the Paris Agreement, every input has uncertainty attached: animal populations, body weights, milk yields, feed digestibility, and the IPCC emission factors themselves. The IPCC 2006 Guidelines (Vol. 1, Ch. 3) ask inventory compilers to quantify how those input uncertainties combine into uncertainty on the reported total, and to report it in the Table 3.3 format.

This calculator does that end to end. It propagates the uncertainty of every parameter through the full IPCC Tier 2 equation chain (IPCC 2006 Vol. 4, Ch. 10 and 11) by Monte Carlo simulation, and returns a defensible 95% confidence interval on your inventory total, a breakdown of how much uncertainty comes from activity data versus emission factors, and a ranking of which parameters would most reduce the uncertainty if you collected better data.

Who is it for?

National GHG inventory compilers preparing Biennial Transparency Reports (BTRs), Biennial Update Reports (BURs) and National Inventory Reports (NIRs) under the UNFCCC Enhanced Transparency Framework, as well as researchers and MRV practitioners working on livestock emissions. If your inventory uses (or is moving to) a Tier 2 characterisation of cattle and you need Approach 2 (Monte Carlo) uncertainty estimates rather than Approach 1 error propagation, this tool was built for you. Two hypothetical example datasets (a dairy system and a pastoral system) are pre-loaded, so you can explore every feature before touching your own data.
