Approach 1 vs Approach 2 uncertainty: which does your inventory need?

The IPCC 2006 Guidelines (Volume 1, Chapter 3) offer two ways to combine input uncertainties into inventory uncertainty. They answer the same question with very different machinery.

Approach 1: error propagation

Closed-form formulas combine percentage uncertainties: sums of sources combine by root-sum-of-squares weighted by magnitude, products (activity data times emission factor) combine by adding squared relative uncertainties. It needs only a spreadsheet, which is why most inventories start there. Its assumptions are strict: roughly normal distributions, uncertainties below about 30% of the mean, no correlation between inputs, and near-linear relationships.

Approach 2: Monte Carlo simulation

Instead of formulas, the inventory is recalculated thousands of times, each time drawing every input from its own probability distribution (normal, lognormal, beta, PERT, triangular and so on). The spread of the simulated totals gives the 95% confidence interval directly. Approach 2 handles what breaks Approach 1: large uncertainties, skewed distributions (emission factors are often lognormal), correlated parameters, and non-linear equation chains such as the IPCC Tier 2 livestock energy-balance model.

IPCC good practice encourages Approach 2 for key categories, and livestock is a key category in most agricultural economies. A Tier 2 cattle inventory is exactly the case Approach 2 exists for: the equation chain is non-linear, several emission factors have wide asymmetric uncertainty, and biologically related parameters co-vary.
